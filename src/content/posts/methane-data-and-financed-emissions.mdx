## Introduction: The New Metric of Financial Risk

For years, banks, asset managers, and investors primarily focused on the direct emissions of their own operations (Scope 1 and 2). Today, that focus has shifted to Financed Emissions—the emissions generated by the loans, investments, and capital they deploy.

Financed Emissions are, for a financial institution, their largest category of Scope 3 emissions (specifically Category 15). They represent the portion of a client's or investee's emissions that the financial institution is financially responsible for.

This metric is now being tracked by global initiatives like the PCAF (Partnership for Carbon Accounting Financials) and numerous Net-Zero alliances, fundamentally redefining financial risk.

## Financed Emissions and the Data Challenge

The vast majority of Financed Emissions originate in high-emitting sectors like oil, gas, and utilities. This creates a critical problem for financial institutions:

**The Data Gap:** To calculate their Financed Emissions accurately, financial firms rely entirely on the emissions data provided by their clients. When clients use Emission Factors (estimates) or non-verifiable data, the resulting Financed Emissions calculation is inherently flawed.

**The Methane Problem:** As a high-impact, short-lived climate pollutant, methane represents a high percentage of the near-term warming potential for energy financing. Inaccurate methane data means the financial sector is blindly underestimating its true climate risk exposure.

## From Estimation to Accountability: The Measurement Imperative

**Setting Quantitative Thresholds:** Engineers can move beyond vague visual inspection criteria. Instead, they can establish a quantitative threshold (e.g., "Any valve in Station B leaking over 4 kg/hr must be scheduled for maintenance in the next 90 days").

**Optimizing Maintenance Windows:** By logging and trending the leak rate over multiple monitoring cycles, maintenance teams can accurately predict when the leak will cross the threshold. This allows repairs to be grouped and scheduled proactively during planned downtime, minimizing costly unplanned shutdowns.

## The Operational Solution

For energy operators, managing Financed Emissions is an opportunity to attract better capital.

**Data as Currency:** By investing in precise methane monitoring systems (moving away from the unreliable Emission Factor methodology), operators provide the transparent data the financial sector needs to meet its own regulatory obligations.

**The Competitive Edge:** Companies that deliver this high-integrity data demonstrate lower climate risk, gain credibility in ESG reporting, and position themselves competitively for favorable financing rates and strategic investment.

## Accurate Measurement is Financial De-risking

The challenge of Financed Emissions means that the pipeline of verifiable methane data must flow cleanly from the field to the balance sheet. For energy operators, providing transparent, measurement-based emissions data is no longer just an environmental goal, it is a critical form of financial de-risking and a requirement for a modern capital relationship.
