**In short:** for the decade 2008–2017, atmospheric "top-down" estimates put global methane emissions at about 576 teragrams of methane per year (Tg CH₄/yr), while "bottom-up" estimates add up to about 737 Tg CH₄/yr. About 60% of the top-down total is caused by human activity.<sup><a href="#source-1">[1]</a></sup>

## The two ways of counting methane

Scientists estimate the global methane budget in two independent ways.

- **Top-down** methods start from measured methane concentrations in the air. Atmospheric inversion models work backwards, using wind and chemistry, to infer how much must have been emitted and where.
- **Bottom-up** methods start from the sources: inventories of human activity (livestock numbers, gas production, landfills) and process models of natural emitters such as wetlands.

## Why the totals disagree

The paper's authors report that the bottom-up total is almost 30% larger than the top-down one. The biggest uncertainty comes from natural emissions, especially wetlands and other inland waters, where bottom-up estimates run higher than the atmosphere appears to allow.<sup><a href="#source-1">[1]</a></sup> That mismatch suggests at least some bottom-up natural emissions are overestimated.

## Why it matters for the climate

- **Concentration:** surface methane reached 1,857 parts per billion (ppb) in 2018, roughly 2.6 times its estimated pre-industrial level in 1750.<sup><a href="#source-1">[1]</a></sup>
- **Warming strength:** over 100 years, methane traps heat about 28 times more effectively than carbon dioxide (GWP100 = 28, IPCC AR5 value, without climate feedbacks).<sup><a href="#source-1">[1]</a>,<a href="#source-2">[2]</a></sup>
- **Short lifetime:** methane lasts about 9 years in the atmosphere, so cutting emissions shows up in concentrations within a few decades.<sup><a href="#source-1">[1]</a></sup>
- **Trend:** since 2012, global methane emissions have been tracking the warmest scenarios assessed by the IPCC.<sup><a href="#source-1">[1]</a></sup>

## What to take away

1. Quote both numbers and name the method; "576" and "737" answer different questions.
2. The gap is mostly a natural-sources problem, which is why better wetland and inland-water maps and more local measurements are priorities.
3. Always keep the unit (Tg CH₄/yr) and the period (2008–2017) with the figure.
